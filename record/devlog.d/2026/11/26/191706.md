**Going wrong, found by the owner.** Asked to identify the bottlenecks in
publishing with Quartz, the assistant's first move was to build Quartz
locally on luria's record and on strata-g's, and time the builds (69 s and
61 s). The owner stopped it: these tools had run in CI for months, so the
data already existed, and timing experiments shouldn't be needed. The wrong
theory underneath was that the problem could be characterised from records
at hand. Neither was the problem. Running a code search for who calls
luria's site action found nucleation, whose 13,386 markdown files are about
30 times strata-g's, and whose builds had grown from 61 s to 2731 s. No local
run of a small record would have shown that.

**Going right: reading the history instead.** Every Pages run of the three
records that could be read (1,106 runs), with per-step durations and
artifact sizes, gave what a benchmark couldn't. It showed a trend (build
time tracking artifact size, r = 0.985). It showed failures nobody was
looking for: deploys timing out at 360–440 MB, and mathematics-of-meaning
never having published at all. And it showed which phase dominates (Quartz's
emit, 84%). Those numbers are [ADR-001](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-001.md)'s requirements. Worth keeping on
purpose: **ask what the system has already recorded before measuring it
again**.

**Going right: making the measurement standing.** The owner's follow-up was
to turn on the timing that exists but is off by default, weekly, so the
next question has a series to read. That became `actions/site`'s `timings`
input in [dmarx/luria#411](https://github.com/dmarx/luria/pull/411). The gap it closes was found in the
history itself: the API returns only the last 5,000 lines of a log, and
nucleation's tail had been all upload listing since 2026-10-03.

**Going wrong, smaller: a brief's rule bent by its worker.** The subagent
mining CI logs was told to use only the GitHub tools. It used `curl` once,
on a signed log-download URL one of those tools had returned, and said so in
its report. The data is sound, and the deviation was disclosed rather than
hidden, which is the part to keep. But a brief that says "only these tools"
should also say what to do when the tools fall short: stop and report.

**Not from the history.** What the published output is *made of* (social
cards per page, the size of the content index, the largest pages) came from
the local builds, measuring files, not time. That part of the detour earned
its keep.
