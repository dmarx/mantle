# ADR-002: mantle adopts nucleation's retrospective process

## Context

nucleation built a retrospective process over 2026-11-20 to 2026-11-23, in
four decisions:

- [NU-ADR-112](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/blob/main/record/decisions.d/ADR-112.md): the POSTMORTEM scheme;
- [NU-ADR-110](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/blob/main/record/decisions.d/ADR-110.md): when a review is due;
- [NU-ADR-115](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/blob/main/record/decisions.d/ADR-115.md): RETRO split from POSTMORTEM, and the devlog as notes for the next
  retrospective;
- [NU-ADR-126](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/blob/main/record/decisions.d/ADR-126.md): earmarks.

Eleven retrospectives and four postmortems later, it is how nucleation
learns from its own work: what went right is recorded as deliberately as
what went wrong, so it is kept on purpose.

mantle is starting from nothing, with an assistant doing much of the work.
That is when a habit is cheapest to set, and when the lessons are least
likely to be written down: a correction the owner makes in conversation is
gone with the session unless something files it.

nucleation's decisions are written for a reading record. Its triggers count
filings and generated entries, and [NU-ADR-110](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/blob/main/record/decisions.d/ADR-110.md) says plainly that its thresholds
are calibrated to nucleation's pace and expected to be superseded. Three of
the four are still Proposed there.

## Decision

*Proposed.* mantle adopts the process. Where the source is still Proposed in
nucleation, adopting it here is mantle's own decision, and doesn't wait on
nucleation's.

**Two schemes,** configured in `luria.yaml` as nucleation has them:

- **RETRO** (`record/retrospectives.d/`): a review of how a change or a
  stretch of work went. Sections: what it covers, what went well and why,
  what did not, recommendations. `reviews`, `prompted`, `pull_requests` and
  `discussions` are all optional.
- **POSTMORTEM** (`record/postmortems.d/`): a review in response to something
  that went wrong. Sections: what happened, why, what it cost, what changes.
  `reviews` and `pull_requests` are required, since a postmortem always
  follows some decision's procedure and some change.
- Both share the statuses Draft, Accepted and Superseded, and are retired by
  status, never deleted. A decision shows the reviews that assessed it
  (`reviewed_by`) and the reviews it carries out (`prompted_by`), both
  derived.

**Notes during the work.** When something seems to be going right or wrong
and worth revisiting, the assistant or the owner files a devlog entry tagged
`retro`, in the pull request of the work it is about. It says what happened,
why it seems to work or not, and any wrong theory and how it was found out.
This needs no asking. A RETRO reads the `retro` entries filed since the
previous RETRO and cites them by date.

**Earmarks**, as [NU-ADR-126](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/blob/main/record/decisions.d/ADR-126.md). When the owner says something is for the
retrospective, in any words, in conversation or on a pull request, the
assistant at once files a note tagged `retro` and `earmarked`. The note
quotes the earmark and says where it was made. The next RETRO answers each
earmarked note by name: kept on purpose, a decision prompted, a change
called for, or set aside and why. An earmark stays open until a RETRO
answers it.

**When a review is due.** These replace [NU-ADR-110](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/blob/main/record/decisions.d/ADR-110.md)'s triggers, keeping its units
(use and sessions, never the calendar):

A **RETRO** is due when:

1. **A contract change has been used.** A change to the manifest schema,
   `luria.yaml`, or a public interface of mantle's build, reviewed once a
   real producer or site has built against it, and before the next change to
   the same contract.
2. **A batch closes.** A working session's work comes to five or more pull
   requests, or includes a stack that had to be folded.
3. **A release is cut**, or a site first moves onto mantle.
4. **The owner asks.**

A **POSTMORTEM** is due when:

5. **A decision is revised soon after landing**, within the session it landed
   or the next.
6. **A process incident happens:** CI red more than once on the same cause, a
   revert, a broken release or published site, or a standing rule broken or
   nearly broken.
7. **The owner asks.**

The assistant checks the triggers when a batch closes and says which are
met. The owner decides whether to write the review; none is filed unasked.
These thresholds are a starting policy. When reviews show they fire too
often or too seldom, this decision is superseded.

## Alternatives considered

- **Only the devlog.** It already holds what was found along the way, but
  nothing makes anyone read it back. A retrospective is the point where the
  notes are read, and where a recommendation becomes a decision.
- **Copy nucleation's triggers verbatim.** "Fifty generated entries" and
  "twenty entries using a field" mean nothing for a software project; a
  trigger nobody can evaluate never fires.
- **Calendar intervals** (a weekly or monthly retro). Rejected in nucleation
  for the reason that holds here: work comes in bursts, and a review on a
  date finds either nothing or too much.
- **Wait until mantle has work worth reviewing.** The first lessons are
  being made now, in its founding sessions. Filing them as they happen is
  the point of the process.

## Consequences

- `luria.yaml` gains the RETRO and POSTMORTEM schemes, their statuses, ADR's
  two derived converses, and nucleation as a remote (`NU`), so its decisions
  are citable by code.
- CLAUDE.md's map names the reviews, the devlog's use, earmarks and the
  trigger check, so an assistant does this without being told.
- **First application, in this pull request:** a devlog note tagged `retro`
  on how the founding requirements were gathered. Gathering them took a
  wrong turn (local timing experiments) that the owner corrected, and the CI
  history that replaced it went right. This decision is itself a contract
  change (trigger 1): it is due for review in the first RETRO written under
  it.
